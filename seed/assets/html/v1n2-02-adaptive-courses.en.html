Adaptive Online Courses and First-Year Students' Academic Performance: A Quasi-Experimental Study

Irina Fedorova, Maxim Kovalenko

  • Lesnogorsk Pedagogical University, Russia

✉ Corresponding author: Irina Fedorova, i.fedorova@example.org

Received: 26 February 2025Accepted: 1 July 2025Published: 25 September 2025

Abstract

The first year at university carries the highest risk of academic failure and dropout, especially in mathematics, where differences in students' school preparation are particularly large. This article evaluates the effect of an adaptive online course in calculus on the performance of first-year students at a pedagogical university. The adaptive system selected exercises and learning materials based on a diagnosis of the student's current level of knowledge and errors. The study used a quasi-experimental design: students in two cohorts of the 2023/24 academic year (n = 214) used the adaptive course, while students in two cohorts of the previous year (n = 198) studied under the traditional scheme with the same instructors. To improve group comparability, propensity score matching was applied on entrance examination results, gender and school type. The mean final examination score in the experimental group was 7.4 points higher out of 100, and the share of students failing the examination at the first attempt fell from 23% to 14%. The largest effect was observed among students with low entrance scores. Analysis of digital traces showed that the key predictor of success was the regularity of work with the system rather than the total time spent in it. We conclude that adaptive courses are a worthwhile tool for levelling first-year students' starting opportunities.

Keywords: adaptive learning; online courses; academic performance; first-year students; learning analytics

1. Introduction

The transition from school to university is accompanied by a sharp change in the demands placed on students’ independence. According to data from many universities, the largest share of students drop out in the first year, and mathematical subjects become the main “filter”. The reason lies largely in the heterogeneity of school preparation, which is difficult to compensate for within whole-class instruction.

Adaptive learning systems, which select tasks according to a student’s individual level, are regarded as a way of approaching the effect of one-to-one tutoring. However, rigorous evaluations of their effectiveness in Russian universities are scarce. The present study estimates the effect of an adaptive calculus course on the performance of first-year students.

2. Materials and Methods

The adaptive course comprised 14 thematic modules and a bank of 1,860 tasks annotated with typical errors. After each task, the system updated its estimate of skill mastery using a Bayesian knowledge tracing model and offered the next task or explanatory material. The course was used as a compulsory part of independent study.

The experimental group consisted of 214 students admitted in 2023, and the control group of 198 students admitted in 2022 who were taught by the same instructors under the same curriculum with traditional homework assignments. Matching was based on a propensity score estimated by logistic regression on total entrance scores, sex and school type; after matching, 181 pairs were included in the analysis.

3. Results

After matching, the mean final examination score was 68.9 in the experimental group and 61.5 in the control group (a difference of 7.4 points; 95% CI: 4.1–10.7). The distribution of the effect across quartiles of entrance scores is shown in Fig. 1: the largest gain is observed in the lowest quartile.

Analysis of the system logs showed that the number of weeks of active work is more strongly associated with the final score (r = 0.52) than the total time spent (r = 0.29). The main results are given in Table 1.

4. Discussion

The effect obtained is comparable with the results of international meta-analyses of adaptive practice in higher education. Its concentration in the group of students with weak preparation indicates that adaptive courses are capable of partially compensating for differences in starting conditions.

The quasi-experimental design does not make it possible to rule out entirely the influence of differences between the intake cohorts of different years that were not accounted for in matching. Nevertheless, the fact that the instructors, curriculum and examination format were the same reduces the risk of bias. The finding on the importance of regular work has a direct practical implication: weekly checkpoints may strengthen the effect.

5. Conclusion

The adaptive online calculus course improved the academic performance of first-year students and reduced the share of unsatisfactory results, especially among students with low entrance scores. It is recommended that adaptive courses be combined with mechanisms that support regular work.
